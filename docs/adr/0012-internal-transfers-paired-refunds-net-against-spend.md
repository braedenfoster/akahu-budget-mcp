# Internal transfers are paired, and refunds net against spend

Before Income Rules or the ADR-0009 fallback run, the classification pass gives each Transaction one of two extra roles. An **Internal transfer** is money moving between two of the Household's own Accounts, detected by **pairing** a debit with an equal and opposite credit on another Account; it is never Income, and it is not spend unless one leg is on a **Savings Account**. A **Refund** is a credit whose description exactly matches a debit from the previous 120 days; it is not Income, takes that debit's Budget Category, and reduces `spent` there. This refines ADR-0009, whose positive-non-`TRANSFER` fallback counted both as Unclassified income: card repayments (a `TRANSFER` on the bank side but a `CREDIT` on the card side) overstated Income by $500–$1,000 a Period, and refunds never came back off spend, so the Spending allowance (ADR-0011) never recovered.

A Savings Account is an Account the Household links to one Budget Category. The leg of a pair that lands on it counts as signed spend in that category: a deposit adds to `spent`, a withdrawal takes it back off. The Savings Envelope therefore shows net money saved, and ADR-0011's rules (Committed underspend withheld, only overspend subtracted) reserve the rest of the target without further logic. This was added after the 2026-10 Period, where a $1,800 automatic payment to SAVINGS (`STANDING ORDER` on BILLS, `CREDIT` on SAVINGS) counted as $1,800 Savings spend, but three withdrawals totalling $1,040 (`TRANSFER` on both legs) were skipped. The Savings Envelope showed an $800 overspend instead of $760 saved, and the Spending allowance read −$875.72 instead of about −$75.72.

## Considered Options

- **"Credits on a credit card are never Income"** — rejected: a repayment that is neither Income nor a `TRANSFER` falls into the spend sum as negative Uncategorized spend, which under ADR-0011 *raises* the Spending allowance by every repayment. Fixing Income this way breaks spend.
- **Transfer rules** (description → internal transfer, like Income Rules) as the primary mechanism — rejected: every new transfer pattern ("oct", "July repay", "credit-card top-up") needs a rule. Pairing needs none and covers transfers between any two Household Accounts. A manual per-Transaction override covers what pairing can't see.
- **Pair after Income Rules** — rejected: once both Accounts are connected, pay that arrives in one Account and moves to another would count as Income twice. Money between the Household's own Accounts is never Income, so pairing runs first.
- **Pair on amount and date alone** — rejected: a salary credit could pair with an unrelated purchase of the same amount. At least one leg must be typed `TRANSFER` or `STANDING ORDER` by Akahu. `STANDING ORDER` is required because Akahu types an automatic payment to savings that way, not as `TRANSFER`.
- **Refund matching by merchant or Akahu category** — not possible: Akahu attaches neither to credits. An exact description match against a recent debit is the only signal available. Fuzzy matching is deferred.
- **Exclude every paired transfer from spend, including savings** — rejected: the Savings Envelope would always show $0 spent, so its whole allocation would be withheld from the Spending allowance even after the money moved, and nothing would show progress.
- **A separate savings-goal model** (account roles, goals with targets, planned replenishments, transfer audit tables) — rejected: it duplicates the Envelope. A Committed Savings Envelope with net `spent` already gives target, net saved, and amount still to move, and ADR-0011 already withholds the gap.
- **Infer Savings Accounts from Akahu's `SAVINGS` account type** — rejected: the bank label does not say what the Household uses the Account for. The Household sets the link.
- **Clamp savings `spent` at zero, or ignore deposits above the allocation** — rejected: money saved beyond the target has left the operating Accounts, so it correctly shows as Committed overspend. Net withdrawals give negative `spent`; under ADR-0011 they never raise the allowance.

## Consequences

- Pairing rules: exactly opposite amounts, two different Household Accounts (any Member), dates within 3 days, at least one leg typed `TRANSFER` or `STANDING ORDER`. Each Transaction is in at most one pair. A pair with both legs transfer-typed wins first, so a savings withdrawal is not taken by a same-day card purchase of the same amount; then the closest date wins, then the lowest id.
- Pairs and refund matches are re-derived from stored data on every categorization pass, so connecting another Account later fixes older Transactions without a re-fetch. A Member override (mark as, or forbid as, internal transfer) survives re-derivation.
- Order of precedence: Member override > pairing > Income Rule > refund match > ADR-0009 fallback.
- The spend sum excludes paired internal transfers, except the leg on a Savings Account. That leg always counts in the linked Budget Category, whatever category it was given, and in the Period of its own date. The other leg is excluded. A transfer between two Savings Accounts counts in both, so the net is zero when they share a Budget Category.
- Refunds need no formula change: they are non-Income signed rows in a Budget Category.
- Interest on a Savings Account stays Income, and withholding tax stays in its Budget Category. Only paired transfers move the Savings Envelope.
- Savings withdrawals are not Income, so they do not raise Household headroom or the Spending allowance. A purchase funded from savings still lands in its own Budget Category. To fund it from savings in the budget, a Member reallocates from the Savings Envelope.
- Transfers that cannot pair because the other Account's history starts later (19 early card repayments in the local DB) need a manual override.
- Unpaired `TRANSFER`-type debits stay excluded from spend, as before. Whether a transfer to someone outside the Household is spend is a separate, unmade decision.
- Gifts, reimbursements and friends paying back are neither pairs nor refunds; they stay as Unclassified income for a Member to classify.
